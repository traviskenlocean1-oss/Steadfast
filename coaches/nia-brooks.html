00:00

Nashville, TN

Coaching For Connection

What I loved about dance as a teenager also pulled me into coaching: the urge to keep moving, experimenting, refining, and growing, all while supported by a community doing the same. Fifteen years later, that spirit still fuels me as a Nashville-based mobility and recovery coach.

My path here hasn’t been the traditional one — I got hurt young, rebuilt my own body in a small town with no real gym, and learned how to coach people through real, daily pain while juggling tight schedules and the pressure I put on myself to get every detail right. Along the way, I discovered that big numbers, fancy gear, and fast results don’t define a member’s progress — it’s how well they move and how they feel on the way there.

Whether it’s a stiff desk worker or a state-level lifter, my approach stays the same: breath first, patience always, grit through it all.

When I’m not coaching, you’ll find me hanging out with my three kids, losing at chess, or seeing if I can still hold a handstand at 36.

Past work includes PT clinics, Nashville SC youth teams, local dance studios, and two running clubs.

Prompts

The first time I ran a full class of twenty on my own. I’d been brought in early enough to help shape the program with the head coach and plan every block so I knew exactly what we needed. Of course, things came up on the day, but we pivoted, I trusted my gut, and pulled it together in the cooldown. I got to coach an amazing group, a loud crowd, a DJ — the works. Even though everyone was working super hard, and I had prepped like crazy, it felt like I was just doing cool stuff with my friends. That combo of camaraderie and grit is why I love this job.

Wrangling my three kiddos, losing at chess, or seeing if I can still hold a handstand at 36.

The Turkish get-up blew me away when I first learned it 14 years ago, and it still does. In modern training, we can be incredibly flashy, throw complex circuits in people's faces, add bands and chains all over the place, and bring tons of energy to a session. And I love that. But this lift reminds me that simplicity can be just as powerful. I also keep going back to the deep goblet squat. The patience, the breathing, the way it shows you exactly where you're stuck… It’s beautiful. I love heavy lifting, and that’s in the background, but it brings the basics of good movement out in a really beautiful way. Yeah, it’s a warm-up move. Sue me.

For me it’s about process, not just chasing a big number. If you value patience, clarity, and a grounded approach to moving well, we could build something great together, one rep at a time.

Lacrosse balls. Single, peanut, spiky, you name it — I’m a sucker for them, and I can almost always be talked into them being the “right” tool for a tight spot. I’m also big on organization: writing session plans helps me clear my head when I’m coaching, and journaling keeps me reflecting on the process.

Easy one. A physical therapist told me at 19 that I would never squat again. I spent two years proving her wrong, and the day I squatted my bodyweight I knew exactly what I wanted to do with the rest of my life.

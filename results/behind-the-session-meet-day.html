Behind the Session: Meet Day

STEADFAST

Oct 8, 2025

A DIFFERENT KIND OF MEET

Most powerlifting meets push the same button: big crowds, loud hype, endless rules. Our first in-house meet with the Barbell Club and the Masters group set out to do the opposite.

Meet day isn’t about rushing from one attempt to the next. It’s a day to slow down. To take your time. Our Masters lifters, led by Michael Tate and Burton Rhodes, brought that idea forward. Our role was to bring it to life in a way that felt like a real competition, but didn’t scare a single first-timer off the platform.

ON THE PLATFORM

Run by Sam Whitfield and judged by coach Joe Sailor, the meet was held across three platforms set up on the main Steadfast floor: one for the squat, one for the bench, and one for the deadlift. Each flight was planned with a playful edge, reminding lifters that a first meet can be just as rewarding as chasing the next big number.

THE PROCESS

The meet came together over two jam-packed days. The first day stretched into what we call a “donut day,” starting at sunrise, breaking for lunch, and coming back for the last flights of the evening. The bench and deadlift ran on a single day, blending family energy with quieter moments by the chalk bowl.

Our crew was lean, working mostly with volunteers and adjusting the schedule as needed. Timing and patience became our biggest tools. Slow, careful warm-ups gave each flight the unhurried feel that defined the whole day.

“Big meets often push spectacle, but our members called for a different rhythm. Our goal was to let the pace of the club itself shape the whole day, from the first warm-up set all the way to the last deadlift of the final flight.”
— Sam Whitfield, Head Coach

THE COMMUNITY

The result is a meet that doesn’t shout for attention. It invites people in. By encouraging lifters to pause, breathe, and take their time, the day reflected the spirit of the club itself.We’re grateful to every member who lifted, judged or loaded plates, and to every family who showed up to cheer. Days like this remind us that, in a fitness industry currently dominated by quick fixes, flashy programs, and in-your-face hype… sometimes slowing down and taking your time is just as sweet.
